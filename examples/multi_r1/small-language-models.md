## Current Landscape of On‑Device Small LMs

*Status: complete; confidence: low.*

- On July 16, 2024, the SmolLM family of small language models was introduced in sizes of 135M, 360M, and 1.7B parameters aimed at operating on local devices. [1]
- Small language model series designed to run on local devices include Microsoft's Phi series, Meta's MobileLLM, Alibaba's Qwen2 (under 2B parameters), and SmolLM. [1]
- SmolLM models were trained on the SmolLM-Corpus, which consists of Cosmopedia v2 (28B tokens of synthetic textbooks and stories generated by Mixtral), Python-Edu (4B tokens from The Stack), and deduplicated FineWeb-Edu (220B tokens). [1]

## Benchmarking Performance on Edge Devices

*Status: complete; confidence: high.*

- On a high‑end smartphone GPU (iPhone 18 Pro GPU), the LiteRT‑LM text generation latency is 11.6 ms per token, enabling roughly 86 tokens per second for on‑device inference. [2]
- On a mid‑range embedded SoC (Android Snapdragon S26 Ultra CPU), LiteRT‑LM text generation latency is 27.1 ms per token, corresponding to about 37 tokens per second. [2]
- On an ultra‑low‑power MCU class device (Raspberry Pi 5), the Qwen 2.5 3B model achieves ~8 tokens per second, which translates to ~125 ms latency per token. [3]
- Gemma 3 4B delivers the best overall edge performance, scoring 43.6 % on the MMLU benchmark and reaching ~27 tokens per second on an iPhone 16 Pro (≈37 ms per token) when run with the Google AI Edge SDK. [3]
- Phi‑4‑Mini (3.8 B) attains the highest reasoning accuracy among the surveyed models, with 88.6 % on GSM8K and 83.7 % on ARC‑C, while generating ~22 tokens per second on a MacBook M4 Air (≈45 ms per token). [3]
- Qwen 2.5 3B provides the strongest quality‑per‑parameter ratio under 4 B parameters, achieving a 65.6 % MMLU score and sustaining ~8 tokens per second on Raspberry Pi 5 (≈125 ms per token). [3]
- The EmbeddingGemma 2 text‑only model occupies 165 MB of storage (270 M parameters), illustrating that sub‑200 MB footprints are feasible for on‑device LMs on edge hardware. [2]
- Publicly reported baseline n‑gram language models lack latency, memory, or accuracy numbers in the surveyed sources, preventing a direct quantitative comparison with the small LMs on the same edge devices. [2][3]

Open questions:
- Exact memory consumption (RAM peak) of the Gemma 3 4B, Phi‑4‑Mini, and Qwen 2.5 3B models on each hardware class is not disclosed in the sources.
- Latency and throughput figures for baseline n‑gram models on the same edge devices are missing, limiting the ability to quantify relative speed‑up.

## Optimization Techniques for On‑Device Deployment

*Status: complete; confidence: low.*

- 4‑bit quantization can dramatically shrink LLMs such as Llama 3.1 while retaining most of the original accuracy. [4]
- The post evaluates five 4‑bit quantization pipelines—AQLM, bitsandbytes, AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound—by measuring inference throughput, accuracy, and memory usage on Llama 3.1. [4]
- Among the methods surveyed, 4‑bit quantization is described as the most popular technique for compressing large language models on‑device. [4]

- Which combinations of quantization (e.g., mixed‑precision) with pruning or distillation yield the best overall performance on typical on‑device hardware classes?
