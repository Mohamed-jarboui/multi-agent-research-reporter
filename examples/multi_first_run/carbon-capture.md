# Carbon Capture: Current Costs and Limits (2026)

**Summary**  
The United States remains the most documented jurisdiction for carbon‑capture policy, centered on the 45Q tax credit and complementary DOE funding. By 2026 more than 270 projects have been announced, yet rising inflation and cost pressures are eroding the credit’s effectiveness. Significant information gaps persist for non‑U.S. policy frameworks, cost‑trajectory modeling, and broader environmental and societal impacts, limiting a comprehensive assessment of global deployment prospects.

## Policy Landscape and Future Cost Trajectories  

- The U.S. Section 45Q tax credit was overhauled and made broadly available to multiple sectors in 2018 [1].  
- Congress enhanced the 45Q credit levels in 2022 to stimulate deployment in higher‑cost sectors such as industry and power generation [1].  
- By 2026 more than 270 carbon‑capture projects have been announced in the United States, with 132 of them in advanced development [1].  
- The 45Q tax credit, together with DOE demonstration and deployment funding, is identified as the primary policy mechanism driving U.S. investment in carbon‑capture technologies [1].  
- The Coalition warns that without updating the 45Q credit to offset rising development and operational costs, future projects—including direct‑air‑capture, carbon‑reuse, and transport‑storage infrastructure—are at risk [1].  
- The 45Q credit has been the lynchpin for deploying carbon‑management technologies across essential domestic industries such as cement, steel, chemicals, natural‑gas‑fired, and coal‑fired power plants [1].  
- The 2022 credit enhancement was intended to catalyze additional deployment in higher‑cost sectors, but inflation and cost pressures have eroded its effectiveness [1].
